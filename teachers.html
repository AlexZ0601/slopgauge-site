What you get

Evidence you can read, not a mystery score.

Every sentence, marked.

Sentences that read as AI-written are shaded, and named tells are underlined. Point at a mark to see why. A gauge shows how much of the essay reads as AI.

Honest odds.

Pick the kind of text, from middle-school essays to college papers and essays by English learners, and Slopgauge tells you how often writing by people scores that high. Its thresholds are set so that about 1 in 100 student essays is called “Likely AI-written”.

Does it sound like them?

Paste something the student wrote in class. A neural style model compares their voice with the essay, part by part, and marks the parts that don’t sound like them.

A whole class at once.

Drop in every essay from an assignment. Slopgauge ranks them, and groups essays that read as AI and share unusual wording: the trace left when several students paste the same prompt into a chatbot.

Reports for the conversation.

Print a report, save it as a PDF, or download the class as a spreadsheet. Each report says what was checked, when, and what the reading can and can’t tell you.

Every student’s history, with Pro.

Save each class on your computer. From then on, each new essay is compared with that student’s own earlier essays, and you see the whole term at a glance. See Pro.

Student privacy

There’s no student data to lose.

Most AI checkers upload each essay to their servers. Slopgauge doesn’t have any. Essays, files and names are read and analyzed in your browser on your computer, and Pro’s saved classes stay there too. There are no accounts, no analytics and no tracking.

That makes Slopgauge simple to approve: there is nothing for it to store, share or sell. If your district still wants paperwork, email us and we’ll help.

No detector can prove who wrote a text, and Slopgauge doesn’t pretend to. Short texts are the hardest: under a few hundred words, results are less reliable. More on the test results.

Using it well

A reading starts a conversation.

  1. Read the marks, not just the number.Which sentences are shaded, and do the named tells match what you noticed?
  2. Compare with their own writing.Paste an in-class essay or an earlier assignment. An essay that reads as AI and doesn’t sound like them is worth a closer look.
  3. Ask about the process.Drafts, notes and Google Docs version history show how a piece was written far better than any detector.
  4. Talk before you decide.Ask the student to walk you through an argument or a word choice. Writers know their own work.
  5. Remember the 1 in 100.Formal, formulaic writing can read as AI. If a student says they wrote it, our guide for flagged writers shows them how to show it.

Teachers ask

Is student work uploaded anywhere?

No. Essays are read and analyzed in your browser, on your computer. Slopgauge has no servers that receive them.

How often does it wrongly flag a student?

On 2,999 essays by students in grades 6 to 12, it called 1 in 100 “Likely AI-written”. It caught 72 of 100 essays written by AI on the same prompts. For other kinds of writing, the checker uses thresholds set for that kind.

Can it prove a student used AI?

No detector can. Treat a high reading as a reason to look closer and to talk with the student, never as proof on its own.

Which files can I check?

Word documents, PDFs and plain text, one at a time or many at once: select every file from an assignment, or drag them all in. You can also paste text.

Does it work with Google Docs?

Yes. With the free Chrome extension, open a student’s Google Doc and the meter appears; its “Full report” opens the essay checker. Or download submissions and drop them into Check a class.

Is it really free?

Yes: the essay checker, comparing with known writing and checking a class are free, with no account. Pro adds saved classes and comparisons with each student’s earlier essays.
