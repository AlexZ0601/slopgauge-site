Field guide

How to spot AI writing.

The 29 signs of AI writing that Slopgauge looks for, each with an example and what to write instead. Every example on this page is flagged by Slopgauge’s detector, and the page is built from the detector’s own rules.

One sign proves nothing. People use all of these. What gives generated text away is a pile-up: several of them, in one short piece, together with very even rhythm and word choice.

Stock words and phrases

01

AI vocabulary

A word chatbots use far more often than people do.

This framework will help your team delve into the data and leverage every insight.

Instead: Use the plain word (“use”, “look at”, “show”), or say the specific thing.

02

Empty phrase

A stock phrase that fills space.

In today's fast-paced world, small businesses need to adapt.

Instead: Cut it. It delays the point.

03

Chatbot leftovers

Leftover phrasing from a chatbot conversation.

Certainly! Here is a revised version of your cover letter. I hope this helps!

Instead: This is chatbot phrasing. Cut it.

04

Importance puffery

Inflates importance instead of stating a fact.

The new policy marks a pivotal moment in the evolving landscape of public health.

Instead: State the fact and let the reader judge whether it matters.

05

Fake-strong verb

"Serves as" or "stands as" where a person would write "is".

The museum serves as a reminder of the city's industrial past.

Instead: Use "is", or say what it actually does.

06

Weasel attribution

Claims authority without naming a source.

Experts agree that remote work is here to stay.

Instead: Name the source, or cut the claim.

07

Telling the reader what to think

Tells you how to read the text instead of saying something.

It's important to note that the results were mixed.

Instead: Delete the aside. If the point is clear, it needs no signpost.

Manufactured drama

08

Binary contrast

Knocks down a claim nobody made, to sound insightful. A signature chatbot move.

It's not just a job. It's a calling.

Instead: Drop the "not X" half and state Y directly.

09

Contrast tails

Ends clause after clause with ", not X", a contrast nobody asked for.

We build for people, not metrics. We ship for users, not awards. We grow through trust, not ads.

Instead: Say what it is; drop the ", not X" tacked onto the end.

10

Negative listing

"Not X. Not Y. Z." A dramatic formula.

Not a sprint. Not a marathon. A lifestyle.

Instead: Skip the "Not X. Not Y." and just say what it is.

11

Dramatic fragments

Short punchy fragments stacked for effect.

We tried everything. Nothing worked. Then we listened. Everything changed.

Instead: Use complete sentences.

12

Colon reveal

Fake suspense: a label, a colon, then the punchline.

The secret to great writing: clarity.

Instead: Rewrite as a plain sentence. Save colons for lists and labels.

13

Self-answered question

Asks a question just to answer it for drama.

The result? A team that finally ships on time.

Instead: Drop the question and make the point.

14

Rhetorical setup

A setup line that builds fake suspense.

Bottom line: if your team can't explain the plan, there isn't one.

Instead: Drop the setup and make the point.

15

Throat-clearing opener

Announces a point instead of making it.

Here's the thing: most meetings could be an email.

Instead: Cut it and state the point.

16

Faux-insight setup

Dresses up an ordinary point as secret knowledge.

Here's what nobody tells you about saving money: start small.

Instead: Cut the setup and let the claim stand on its own.

17

Fake-profound kicker

A fake-profound closing line.

She kept every receipt for ten years. And that makes all the difference.

Instead: Delete it and end on your clearest concrete sentence.

Shape and rhythm

18

Rule of three

Lists of three, over and over.

Our team values honesty, curiosity, and courage. We build fast, test often, and learn always. The result is speed, quality, and trust.

Instead: Three-item lists everywhere read as generated. Keep the items that matter.

19

Repetitive openers

Short sentences in a row that start or end the same way.

We tested the app with real users last spring. We changed the onboarding twice. We cut the price in half for students. We now have twelve paying schools.

Instead: Short sentences in a row that start or end the same way. Vary them, or merge them.

20

Essay transitions

Stacked essay transitions like "Moreover" and "Furthermore".

Moreover, the plan saves money. Furthermore, it saves time. Additionally, it is simple to run. Consequently, we should adopt it.

Instead: Several formal transitions in one piece reads as generated. Most can go.

21

Superficial analysis

A trailing -ing clause that sounds like analysis but adds none.

The city opened three new libraries in 2024, highlighting its commitment to education.

Instead: Replace the -ing clause with the concrete consequence.

22

Summary-recap ending

Restates what you just read.

In conclusion, clear communication, steady habits and real feedback are the keys to growth.

Instead: The reader was just there. End on the last concrete point.

23

Em dash

Em dashes all over a short post are a common chatbot tell.

We launched in March — and the response was huge — bigger than we ever expected — truly.

Instead: Use a comma, a period or parentheses. One dash in a piece is fine; a dash in every sentence is a habit.

Social-post habits

24

Engagement bait

Fishing for comments and reposts.

What do you think? Drop a comment below and repost if you agree.

Instead: Cut it. A good post gets replies without asking.

25

Emoji bullets

Emoji used as bullet points, typical of templated posts.

Three wins this week:
✅ Shipped the beta
✅ Hired our first engineer
✅ Closed a pilot

Instead: Write the points as sentences, or use plain bullets.

26

Arrow bullets

"X → Y" lines instead of sentences, a templated-post habit.

What I learned:
→ Listen first
→ Lead with empathy
→ Celebrate small wins

Instead: Write the point as a sentence instead of "X → Y".

27

Fancy Unicode text

Bold or italic lookalike letters from a post-formatting tool.

𝐁𝐢𝐠 𝐧𝐞𝐰𝐬: we just raised our seed round.

Instead: Use plain text. Screen readers and search skip these lookalike letters.

28

Label-colon lines

A stack of "Label: explanation" lines, the shape of a pasted chatbot answer.

Strategy: focus on one market at a time.
Execution: ship something small every week.
Culture: hire people who stay curious.
Result: steady growth without burnout.

Instead: Several "Label: explanation" lines in a row read like a pasted chatbot answer. Write it as prose.

29

Pasted formatting

Chatbot formatting (** or #) pasted somewhere it does not render.

**Key insight:** customers want speed.
## Next steps
Ship the faster checkout first.

Instead: This site shows the symbols literally. Remove them.
