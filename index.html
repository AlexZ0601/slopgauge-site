A quieter feed

Slop level guide

No slop

Nothing that reads like chatbot writing.

A little slop

One pattern. People write like this too.

Some slop

Two patterns.

Heavy slop

Three or four. Rare in writing people do by hand.

Pure slop

Five or more.

We tested it on 668 news articles written from the same headlines: 268 by journalists and 400 by AI models. 21% of the AI-written articles scored Heavy slop or worse. None of the human-written ones did. A repeated habit counts once, so a long post isn't flagged just for being long.

Ingredients

Ingredients: chatbot vocabulary (delve, leverage, a testament to, serves as), binary contrasts (“It's not a tool. It's a teammate.”), throat-clearing openers (“Here's the thing:”), colon reveals (“The best part: it's free.”), self-answered questions (“The result? Growth.”), engagement bait (“Agree?”), chatbot leftovers (“I hope this helps!”, “[Your Name]”), negative listings, fake-profound kickers, weasel attribution, importance puffery, rule-of-three lists, and about fifteen more.

Contains: em dashes, emoji bullets, pasted markdown.

Storage and handling

Keep on your device. Contains no servers.

Slopgauge reads the text in your browser and never sends anything you read to anyone. There is no account and no analytics.

On Google Docs, which draws its text on a canvas, it downloads the open doc as plain text from Google, the same as File > Download. That is the only network request it makes.
