The tells, by name.

A score alone doesn’t help. Slopgauge points at the habits that make chatbot writing sound like chatbot writing, about thirty of them, and a model trained on 120,000 texts weighs them with word choice and rhythm.

Binary contrast n.
“It’s not a tool. It’s a teammate.”
Knocking down a claim nobody made, to sound decisive.
Throat-clearing n.
“Here’s the thing: most meetings should be emails.”
Announcing a point instead of making it.
Colon reveal n.
“The best part: it’s free.”
A drumroll before an ordinary fact.
Chatbot words pl. n.
“Let’s delve into this rich tapestry.”
Words people rarely reach for: delve, tapestry, leverage, a testament to.
Engagement bait n.
“Agree? Drop a comment below.”
A last line written for the algorithm, not the reader.
Leftovers pl. n.
“Certainly! Here’s a post about my promotion:”
Text pasted straight out of the chat window.

Plus negative listings, fake-profound kickers, rule-of-three lists, em dash pileups, emoji and arrow bullets, weasel attribution and more.
