Slop level guide

No slop

Reads like writing people do by hand.

A little slop

Some chatbot habits. About 1 in 10 human posts lands here too.

Some slop

Clearly templated. About 3 in 100 human posts.

Heavy slop

Rare in writing people do by hand: about 1 in 100.

Pure slop

About 3 in 1,000 human posts.

The level comes from a small model trained on about 120,000 posts, tweets, essays and articles by people and by GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini, Llama and others, which weighs word choice and rhythm together with the named patterns. On posts it had never seen, with 5% of human posts flagged, it caught 59% of AI-written social posts and 95% of AI-written Reddit posts, and flagged none of the human tweets or English learners' essays. Turn on Deep check in the popup to add a larger neural model on your computer.

Ingredients

Ingredients: chatbot vocabulary (delve, leverage, a testament to, serves as), binary contrasts (“It's not a tool. It's a teammate.”), throat-clearing openers (“Here's the thing:”), colon reveals (“The best part: it's free.”), self-answered questions (“The result? Growth.”), engagement bait (“Agree?”), chatbot leftovers (“I hope this helps!”, “[Your Name]”), negative listings, fake-profound kickers, weasel attribution, importance puffery, rule-of-three lists, and about fifteen more.

Contains: em dashes, emoji bullets, pasted markdown.

Checking a whole essay?

Paste it into the essay checker for a full report.

It marks the sentences that read as AI-written, tells you how much of the text does, and lists the patterns behind it. Free, and it runs in your browser like the extension: nothing you paste is uploaded.

Made with teachers in mind, and honest about its limits: about 1 in 100 human-written essays is still called "Likely AI-written", so a report is a reason to talk with the writer, not proof.

Storage and handling

Keep on your device. Contains no servers.

Slopgauge reads the text in your browser and never sends anything you read to anyone. There is no account and no analytics.

On Google Docs, which draws its text on a canvas, it downloads the open doc as plain text from Google, the same as File > Download. That is the only network request it makes.
