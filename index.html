    Questions

    Good to know.

    Can Slopgauge prove that someone used AI?

    No, and no detector can. A high reading means a text shares habits that are common in AI-written text. About 1 in 100 school essays written by students is still called “Likely AI-written”. Treat a reading as a reason to look closer or to have a conversation, never as proof on its own.

    How accurate is it?

    On 2,999 essays by students in grades 6 to 12, the essay checker called 1 in 100 “Likely AI-written” and caught 72 of 100 essays written by AI on the same prompts. Short texts are harder: under a few hundred words, results are less reliable. See the test results.

    Is my text uploaded anywhere?

    No. The extension and the essay checker analyze text in your browser and send it nowhere. The one exception is Google Docs, which draws its text on a canvas: Slopgauge downloads the open doc as plain text from Google, the same as File > Download, and reads it locally. Read the privacy policy.

    Is it free?

    Yes. The extension and the essay checker are free, with no account, no ads and no trial.

    Which browsers does it work in?

    The extension works in Chrome, and in browsers that install extensions from the Chrome Web Store, such as Microsoft Edge and Brave. The essay checker works in any modern browser, on a computer or a phone.

    What counts as slop?

    Text that reads as generated rather than written: stock openers like “In today’s fast-paced world”, “It’s not X, it’s Y” contrasts, rule-of-three lists, self-answered questions, and posts that end by fishing for comments. Slopgauge names about thirty of these habits and weighs them with word choice and sentence rhythm.

    I’m a teacher. How should I use it?

    Paste or drop an essay into the essay checker, pick the kind of text, and read the marked sentences. Paste something the student wrote in class to check whether each part sounds like them. Check a class measures a whole assignment at once and groups essays that read as AI and share unusual wording.

    Then talk with the student. A reading is where a conversation starts, not a verdict.

    Why was my own writing flagged?

    Formal, formulaic writing can share AI’s habits: stock transitions, very even sentences, a five-paragraph shape. That is the 1 in 100. Short texts are flagged more often too. The named tells show exactly which phrases tipped the reading.

    About

    Made by a student who wants the internet to sound human again.

    Slopgauge is an independent project by a student at Princeton University. It started with a simple frustration: feeds, inboxes and group chats were filling up with posts nobody had really written, and there was no quick, honest way to tell.

    The detectors that existed uploaded your text, returned a single number and never said why. So Slopgauge works the other way around. It runs on your own device, it shows the evidence behind every reading, and it publishes how often it gets things wrong.

    It’s built out of genuine passion for the problem, and it’s free: no ads, no accounts and no data to sell. Ideas, bug reports and readings that look wrong are always welcome at support@slopgauge.com.

    Built on open research

    Slopgauge stands on public datasets and models shared by researchers:

    • The style modelTrained on about 120,000 texts from openly licensed research corpora: DACTYL, MAGA, LLMTrace, LLMDetect, Ghostbuster and TweetEval.
    • Deep checkVanguard, an open neural AI-text detector, run on your own computer.
    • Voice comparisonLUAR, from Learning Universal Authorship Representations (Rivera-Soto et al., EMNLP 2021).
    • Accuracy testingPERSUADE 2.0, a corpus of essays by students in grades 6 to 12, used only to measure accuracy and set thresholds.
